# Writing rules

Single source of truth for Felipe's LinkedIn voice. The Content Reviewer
scores posted posts against it. The Strategist and Editor apply the parts
marked **[ideas]** to proposed topics.

## Purpose

- Stay active for the LinkedIn algorithm and show knowledge. Do not chase
  attention or reach. **[ideas]**
- Content is for senior data engineers: advanced topics, no hype, no trends
  for their own sake. **[ideas]**

## Framing

- Lead with the problem or the lesson, not a brag. "A mistake I made / a
  problem I hit / a decision I had to make, and what I learned" reads as
  useful. "Look what I built" reads as self-promotional. **[ideas]**
- Structure: setup -> problem -> what I did or got wrong -> result. End on the last
  fact or on what is still unresolved. Grounded and professional.
- Narrow scope: one decision, not a topic overview. **[ideas]**

## Clarity

Senior topic, simple words. A data engineer who has never used this tool
should get the problem, the cause and the fix on one read.

- Write one plain sentence first, for yourself: what happened, why, what
  fixed it, with no product terms. If it can't be written, the post isn't
  ready. **[ideas]** (the topic's core must fit in one plain sentence)
- Give the setup in the first paragraph: what I was trying to do, in
  plain words. Then what broke, then what I found, then the fix. One job
  per paragraph.
- One idea per post. Drop detours (a wrong guess gets one sentence, not a
  paragraph).
- Explain every tool-specific term the first time in a few plain words
  ("the driver, the node that coordinates the work").
- At most one raw identifier (error code, API name, setting name) in the
  post. Describe the others in words.
- Paragraphs of at most ~70 words. Most sentences under 20 words.
- One plain "so" sentence that says what a fact means is allowed. It is
  not a lesson ending.

## Length and format

- At most three paragraphs. Short and skimmable.
- Vary length: sometimes ~80 words, sometimes ~220. Do not always land near 200.
- Active voice. Contractions and fragments are fine.
- Mostly short sentences, with an occasional longer one (up to ~25
  words) for rhythm.
- No emojis. No em dashes or double hyphens. No all-caps.

## Substance

- Concrete over abstract: specific numbers, tool names, real outcomes.
  **[ideas]** (the idea must allow real numbers from a reproducible test)
- Numbers are used sparingly and in service of explaining a decision, not
  as a highlight reel.
- Do not pair every metric with a second metric for contrast.
- No fabricated numbers or scenarios. **[ideas]**
- No employer data, client names or internal tooling. Personal projects or
  safely generalized patterns only. **[ideas]**

## Banned (these make posts read as generated)

- "The trade-off is..." / "The decision comes down to..." / "comes down to"
  as a closer.
- The "X is not Y, it is Z" negation-reversal construction, in any form.
- Ending by generalizing past the tool ("this isn't specific to Fabric",
  "any engine can do this"). Allowed in at most one post out of three, and
  only when Felipe says so.
- Opening with "I built / I tested / I reviewed / I needed to".
- Closing with a lesson or takeaway, unless Felipe asked for one. Posts end
  after the fact or on something unresolved.

## Avoid (influencer patterns)

- Engagement-bait openers: "This changed everything", "Nobody talks about
  this". **[ideas]**
- Engagement-bait questions to farm comments ("Agree? 👇").
- Humble-bragging about promotions, salary or credentials.
- Motivational quotes unrelated to the field.
- Viral formats for reach: rage-bait takes, "unpopular opinion" posts. **[ideas]**
- Emoji-heavy or all-caps formatting.

## Topic sentence rules (Schedule table)

- One sentence, at most ~25 words.
- States the problem, failure or decision, with the specific tool or setting.
- No hooks or bait wording; it is a planning note, not a headline.
- At most 1 in 4 topics framed as "X vs Y".
